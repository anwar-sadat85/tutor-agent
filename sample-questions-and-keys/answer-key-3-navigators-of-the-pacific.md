# Answer Key — Navigators of the Pacific

Notes for grading: proper nouns (waka hourua, Hōkūle'a, kūmara, taro) should be accepted with reasonable spelling variation given handwriting — do not penalise minor spelling differences on these terms as long as intent is clear.

1. **Literal.** Any two of: stars, the sun's path, ocean swells, wave patterns, bird flight paths.

2. **Inferential.** Certain seabirds fly out to feed in the morning and return to land in the evening, so their flight direction could point toward land before it was visible.

3. **Literal.** A double-hulled voyaging canoe; its design made it stable in rough seas and able to carry enough people, plants, and animals for a new settlement.

4. **Vocabulary.** "Apprentice" / "apprentices."

5. **Inferential/opinion.** Open-ended — accept reasonable reasoning, e.g. that hands-on practice was needed to truly master reading the ocean, or that oral traditions were the normal way of passing on knowledge at the time. No single correct answer; grade on quality of reasoning.

6. **Literal.** Kūmara, taro, dogs (accept "and more" / general reference to plants and animals for a new settlement).

7. **Literal.** A single mistake could mean missing an island entirely.

8. **Inferential.** To prove the ancient knowledge still works and to make sure it isn't lost. Accept reasonable paraphrasing.
