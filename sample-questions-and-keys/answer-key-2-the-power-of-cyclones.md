# Answer Key — The Power of Cyclones

Notes for grading: this is a nonfiction/informational passage, so answers should be checked against the facts stated in the text rather than open interpretation, except for Q8 which allows some reasoning.

1. **Literal.** November to April.

2. **Literal/inferential.** The rising warm, moist air creates low pressure, and cooler air rushes in to fill the gap; the Earth's spin pushes this air into a spiral. Accept an answer that captures either the low-pressure cause or the Earth's-spin cause, ideally both.

3. **Literal.** Because New Zealand is far south and the surrounding water is too cool for the cyclone to survive at full strength.

4. **Vocabulary.** "Cluster."

5. **Literal.** A weakened cyclone that still produces heavy rain but without the fierce winds of a full cyclone.

6. **Literal.** Any two of: satellites, weather balloons, ocean buoys.

7. **Inferential.** Accept any two of: time to prepare sandbags, time to secure outdoor furniture, time to evacuate low-lying areas, general safety/preparedness.

8. **Inferential.** Should reference that rising ocean temperatures may let cyclones hold more moisture and travel further before weakening, meaning stronger/more impactful storms could reach NZ more often. Accept reasonable paraphrasing that captures this cause-and-effect link.
