# Reading Comprehension — Year 6
## The Lost Track

Read the passage carefully, then answer the questions in full sentences.

---

Mia adjusted the straps on her backpack and looked back at the car park, now just a smudge of grey between the trees. She had walked this track with her dad a dozen times, but today he had let her go ahead while he tied his bootlace, and somewhere in the last ten minutes she had taken a wrong turn.

The bush around her was thick with ferns, and the path beneath her feet had narrowed to little more than a rabbit trail. She stopped and listened. No voices, no footsteps — just the creak of branches and the distant call of a tūī. Her heart began to beat a little faster, but she remembered what her dad always said: *if you're lost, stop before you go any further.*

She sat down on a fallen log and pulled out her water bottle. Panicking wouldn't help. She thought back over her steps. The track had split at a big rimu tree with a carving on its trunk — she was sure of that. If she followed her own footprints back through the soft mud, she might find it again.

Slowly, carefully, Mia retraced her path, checking the ground for her own boot prints. After a few minutes, she spotted the carved rimu tree exactly where she remembered it. Relief flooded through her. She had barely started back down the correct track when she heard her dad's voice calling her name, closer than she expected.

"There you are!" he said, jogging up to her, his face pale with worry. "I turned around and you were gone."

"I took a wrong turn," Mia admitted, "but I remembered what you taught me. I stopped, and I followed my own footprints back."

Her dad let out a breath and pulled her into a hug. "That," he said, "is exactly what you're supposed to do."

---

### Questions

1. Why did Mia end up walking ahead of her dad?

2. What clue helped Mia realise she had taken a wrong turn?

3. What advice had Mia's dad given her about being lost, and why do you think this is good advice?

4. Find a word in the passage that means "found again by looking back over."

5. How do you think Mia felt when she first realised she was lost? Use evidence from the text to support your answer.

6. Why did Mia decide to sit down instead of continuing to walk?

7. What was the "big rimu tree" important for in the story?

8. In your own words, explain what Mia did to solve her problem.
