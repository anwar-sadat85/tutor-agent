# Answer Key — The Lost Track

Notes for grading: accept reasonable paraphrasing. Full sentences not strictly required if the answer is clearly correct and complete in meaning. Where "evidence from the text" is requested, look for a relevant quote or reference, not just a general statement.

1. **Literal.** Her dad stopped to tie his bootlace and let her walk ahead. (Accept any answer capturing that her dad paused/fell behind while she kept walking.)

2. **Literal.** The path had narrowed to little more than a rabbit trail / it no longer looked like the track she knew.

3. **Inferential.** His advice was: "if you're lost, stop before you go any further." Good answers explain that stopping prevents you from getting more lost, and gives you time to think clearly rather than panic. Accept any reasonable justification along these lines.

4. **Vocabulary.** "Retraced" (from "retraced her path").

5. **Inferential.** Accept answers describing nervousness/fear/anxiety, supported by evidence such as "her heart began to beat a little faster" or the description of the quiet bush. Should reference the text, not just state a feeling.

6. **Literal/inferential.** So she could think calmly instead of panicking — connects to "Panicking wouldn't help."

7. **Literal.** It was the landmark where the track had split, so finding it again showed her she was back on the right path.

8. **Summary/inferential.** She stayed calm, sat down to think, remembered a landmark, and followed her own footprints back to it. Accept any answer covering the key steps: stopping, thinking, retracing steps.
