# Answer Key — The Woman Who Mapped the Seafloor

Notes for grading: Q5 and Q8 involve inference/opinion about historical context — grade on quality of reasoning and use of text evidence, not a single "correct" phrasing.

1. **Literal.** That it was flat and featureless.

2. **Literal.** Because women were rarely allowed onto research ships at the time.

3. **Literal.** A long, deep valley running down the middle of an underwater mountain range.

4. **Vocabulary.** "Rift."

5. **Inferential.** Other scientists dismissed her theory, calling it "girl talk," and considered continental drift "fringe science." Accept answers noting this shows gender bias/sexism in science at the time, and that she faced ridicule or lack of credibility despite good evidence.

6. **Literal.** The theory that the Earth's crust is made of moving plates that shift, collide, and pull apart over millions of years.

7. **Vocabulary.** "Refining."

8. **Opinion/inferential.** Open-ended. Accept reasonable answers such as: it corrects an historical injustice, credits her actual scientific contribution, or encourages other overlooked scientists/women in science. Judge on reasoning quality, not a fixed answer.
